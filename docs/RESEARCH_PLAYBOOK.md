# Repeatable research playbook

Version 1 — 2026-10-16. This is the living procedure for water, manufacturing, and smart grids. Update it as we learn from actual work. The [roadmap](RESEARCH_ROADMAP.md) explains the lifecycle; this file explains how to repeat each step. Each project's `docs/WORK_LOG.md` records what actually happened; its `STATUS.md` is the current handoff.

Reuse the process while independently checking each sector's papers, data, assumptions, and contribution. The existence of this checklist does not authorize downloads, installations, experiments, external messages, or publication beyond the user's current instructions.

Apply the confirmed [publication, writing and title standards](PUBLICATION_STANDARDS.md) across all three sectors. Leading-journal ambitions guide the rigor of the study; publication and professional-use claims must remain supported by actual evidence.

## Start and finish every session

### Preserve the intended contribution while learning from feasibility work

**Planning correction applied in manufacturing, 17 October 2026; no new scientific contribution validated.** See the [post-PDF decision](../manufacturing/docs/ORIGINAL_CONTRIBUTION_DIRECTION_V0.1.md).

1. Record the intended paper's contribution objective separately from each pilot's completion criterion. A loader, negative experiment or well-formatted report does not fulfill an original-method objective.
2. Before proposing an extension, connect a baseline claim to its manuscript, code/configuration, assumptions and a fair test. Distinguish a verified implementation/protocol issue from a measured method failure; reproducing our own controls does not reproduce another paper.
3. Demonstrate the relevant limitation, check close prior art and specify why the proposed change should address it. Fixing leakage or adding a familiar component is not automatically algorithmic novelty. Keep common inputs and supervision fair.
4. If current results support only a narrow feasibility report, preserve and label that report, then return to contribution selection. Do not silently redefine it as the user's intended final paper. A revised mechanism needs suitable independent evidence and a new prospective protocol after holdout exposure.
5. Update mutable briefs/status/roadmaps with an explicit superseding decision; retain old results and consumed scientific specifications. Select additional datasets for the proposed claim, not merely because they are accessible. Reuse this discipline in the other sectors without copying their question, methods or completion state.

For a contribution/evidence-scope milestone, use the procedure below before committing to expensive experiments. Manufacturing has executed this **research/design procedure**, not a broad empirical validation.

### Reconciling a proposed contribution with prior art and evidence breadth

Prerequisites: a precise operational question, actual permitted inputs/trust boundary, current model equations, data-unit/annotation audit, development results and protected-test rules.

1. Write a claim-intent manifest before synthesis. Split claims about new mathematics, useful mechanisms, empirical detection, physical correspondence and generalization. List what existing observations cannot establish.
2. Investigate the closest mechanisms through primary full manuscripts, publisher/proceedings metadata and author code. Include older foundational filtering/alignment/surprise work as background; do not hide it behind a recent-paper cutoff. Record versions, equations/sections, dates, licenses, access failures and search scope.
3. Compare exact information access and score algebra. Identify simpler explanations and controls; distinguish changes to score, estimator, inputs and fitting. A renamed divergence or phase variable is not itself novelty. Source absence in a bounded search is not proof of originality.
4. Map claims to independent evidence units: acquisition, session, machine, task and condition. Separate rows/windows/seeds from replications, successor releases from independent captures, cyber interventions from faults and benign changes. Preserve label and clock uncertainty.
5. Select a narrow defensible claim or identify concrete additional evidence required. Check public provider metadata/manuscripts before bulk acquisition; record source-specific contracts, terms, native clocks, group splits and refitting/transfer distinctions. Never copy a warmup or learned reference into a new source without checking physical meaning.
6. Specify the minimum fair comparison by scientific role, with explicit design/implementation/fitting/calibration/final-admission states. Retain strong simple methods and negative findings. Keep the primary contrast fixed; additional scores explain outcomes, not create post-test winner choices.
7. Record falsification and interpretation before final exposure. Joint detection/false-alarm outcomes may reveal a trade-off. If existing methods explain the mechanism or simple controls explain results, revise the claim. A case study can be useful without supporting a general-method or leading-journal claim.
8. Obtain an internal critique, resolve concrete issues and preserve the review's limits. Update current scope/status/roadmap and work log; preserve immutable historical protocols, configurations and runs. Clarify obsolete terminology in a versioned mapping rather than silently editing consumed artifacts. Verify hashes/local links and whitespace; do not rerun unchanged software merely for documentation work.

Expected artifacts: claim-to-prior-art/evidence matrix, scientific comparison roster, independent-source screen/admission plan, query/source receipts, review dispositions, updated handoff and guardrail verification. Completion means a defensible **design decision**, not proven contribution, new data admission or execution approval.

Executed example: manufacturing [scope decision](../manufacturing/docs/CONTRIBUTION_AND_EVIDENCE_SCOPE.md), [roster](../manufacturing/docs/COMPARISON_ROSTER_DECISION.md) and [independent-evidence plan](../manufacturing/docs/INDEPENDENT_EVIDENCE_PLAN.md), 17 October 2026. Reuse the process in water/grids, not its dataset assumptions, selected controls, results or completion status.

### Extending scores without changing a fitted observer

**Executed/validated in manufacturing, 17 October 2026:** [four-score extension report](../manufacturing/docs/SCORE_EXTENSION_REPORT_V0.1.md). Reuse this procedure, not its model, data counts or results, in another project.

1. Freeze the existing model/scaler, source implementation, data/evaluation contracts, dependency hashes and previous run/attempt records. Specify new scores and their scientific roles before protected outcomes; no new score weights or threshold selection is implied.
2. Add a pure score module around the same prospective proposal. Separate proposal creation, score emission and state commit; preserve exact old-score arithmetic and state transitions. Declare numerical support, zero limits, log-domain calculations and tolerances.
3. Test independent arithmetic, high-precision examples, underflow/overflow, immutable state, feature/label isolation and malformed inputs using explicitly synthetic fixtures. Test the new runner against synthetic prior artifacts and preserve failure diagnostics. Bind a fresh complete test receipt to the actual source inventory/interpreter.
4. Declare one bounded no-fit replay on permitted normal data. Preserve warmup, gaps and exact support. Use prior score arrays/coordinates as strict compatibility checks, never as tuning targets. Record source hashing, positioning traversal and selected observation parsing separately.
5. Enforce actual wall/CPU/RSS/output caps and one-attempt records. Resolve monitoring restrictions without silently disabling monitoring. Save provenance, all scores, source/model pre/post checks and failures; do not auto-retry a real failure.
6. Independently read back saved artifacts: complete hashes/lengths, finite values, exact old-score/index parity, algebraic cross-score bounds, immutable inputs, resource receipts and attempt ledger. State which formulas cannot be independently reconstructed without additional retained histories.
7. Preserve the original documents/artifacts as historical snapshots. Record current readiness in a new report/registry and handoff, not by rewriting a consumed plan. A successful extension admits software/normal replay only; calibration, final tests and scientific claims retain separate gates.

### Session record procedure

1. Select the active project and bounded task from the user's instruction or its recorded next step. Read root and project `AGENTS.md`, its brief, status, and latest work-log entries.
2. Check prerequisites in the relevant step below. Continue authorized independent work if an external dependency is pending; record the blocker without declaring the whole step finished.
3. Execute only the scoped work. Save source evidence and artifacts in the active project. For commands needed to reproduce a result, record the exact working directory, command, configuration, versions, and output location. Mark proposed commands as **not run**.
4. Append a dated entry using the template below. Record unsuccessful attempts and protocol changes as well as successes. Keep access credentials, signed download links, restricted data, and private identifiers out of shared documentation.
5. Update the project's `STATUS.md`, then update this playbook only when there is a generalizable procedure or lesson. A change to shared workflow belongs in the [root work log](WORK_LOG.md).

## Lessons already established

- Teach core concepts before detailed model implementation using one explicitly synthetic sector example: physical process → expected measurement → residual/score → threshold → alarm → separate evaluation. Distinguish baseline/ablation, data roles, state/parameter/threshold updates, and hidden labels versus available observations. Use a small worked confusion matrix and explain why test-driven choices bias evaluation. Save assistant teaching material separately from the researcher's own unanswered note; require their actual explanation before recording understanding or reading. Water's [Step 0 lesson](../water/docs/RESEARCH_BASICS_LESSON.md) is a prepared teaching example, not a validated learning outcome. Adapt the example and verify each other sector's physical assumptions independently.
- Dataset names alone do not establish comparability. Resolve the exact release, preprocessing, time ordering, labels, and accessible inputs before selecting the final comparison.
- Audit checkpoint selection, threshold search, and final metric reporting separately. Water's CAROTS audit found different test-based best-F1 paths; changing one does not establish a leak-free evaluation. See [water evidence](../water/docs/literature/CAROTS_AND_INFORMATION_THEORETIC.md).
- A public listing or repository is a lead until authorization, contents, provenance, and usability are checked. A simulator, bus case, architecture framework, or fault record is not automatically an attack dataset or runnable detector.
- Keep setup checks, synthetic tests, measured benchmark results, and published paper scores explicitly distinct. Never mark a sibling project's stage complete because water completed a similar step.
- Select per-sector decisions explicitly: water requires both SWaT and WADI; manufacturing needs production/run semantics; grids need electrical/topology and scenario semantics. These are different inputs to the same audit process.
- Separate baseline implementation/development from final benchmark scoring. Freeze the proposed method, all variants and analysis before inspecting final held-out outcomes. Designing an improvement around baseline test failures makes later results on the same attacks exploratory; a different seed is not independent confirmation. Water's [detailed roadmap](../water/docs/RESEARCH_ROADMAP.md) is a planning example, not evidence that its future steps have been executed.
- Select learning papers separately from execution-ready baselines. Use evidence-based admission rules and complementary scientific roles, rather than filling a fixed quota or choosing the largest published score. Water's [selection criteria](../water/docs/BASELINE_SELECTION_CRITERIA.md) provide a reusable example; substitute each sector's own task, data pair and trusted-input requirements, and verify all evidence independently.
- Preserve a dated search record with exact queries, primary-source locators, exclusions and unresolved facts. Pin code when making detailed implementation claims; distinguish author text, direct code observations, third-party issue reports and our inferences. Failed browser PDF retrieval can sometimes be resolved by reading an authorized public manuscript in memory; record its hash and retrieval procedure, then append a correction to the earlier access limitation. Water's [expanded audit](../water/docs/literature/EXPANDED_SEARCH_2026-10-16.md) demonstrates the source-audit procedure, not reproducibility of its methods.
- Build one resource pack per selected method after collection is authorized. Inventory publisher/preprint versions, code license and pinned tree, supplements, figure/table sources, references, models, scores and logs separately. Download only identified relevant files; a full repository archive may silently include unrelated or restricted datasets. Preserve original bytes, URL/version/time/size/hash and extraction provenance, keep artifacts ignored, and inspect downloads without executing code or deserializing checkpoints. A public TeX archive is manuscript source, not model code; a component checkpoint is not necessarily the detector checkpoint. Record unavailable resources and release/access blockers instead of calling the pack complete. Water's [collection](../water/baselines/README.md) and WATER-007 demonstrate this bounded procedure with public artifacts; they do not demonstrate dataset acquisition or model reproduction. Reapply the procedure independently to manufacturing/grids using their own providers and releases.
- Audit resource packs on separate axes: publication identity/status, file integrity, accuracy of our extraction, internal scientific consistency, reference identity/support and execution readiness. Check original plot curves against captions/decision rules; recompute table arithmetic and formulas under their stated assumptions; trace selection, alignment and checkpoint paths without running untrusted code. Inventory rendered references separately from unused bibliography records, compare DOI/title/authors/venue against primary metadata, and inspect consequential citation support in the actual source. Correct our notes while preserving originals. A genuine article, matching hash, valid syntax or resolved DOI is not proof of correct experiments; access failure is not proof of fabrication. Water's [verification report](../water/docs/BASELINE_VERIFICATION_REPORT.md) and WATER-008 demonstrate this audit with explicit coverage and limits. Carry the procedure, not its verdicts, to the other sectors.
- Study one method from input to alarm using its versioned manuscript and pinned code. Map data preparation, model fitting, objective, checkpoint selection, scores, calibration and reporting to actual functions and line ranges. Trace tensor dimensions, indexing, reductions and configuration overrides; a matching class name does not prove equation fidelity. With no matched code, record an implementation contract of inputs, outputs, retained state and unknown choices, explicitly labelled conceptual. Separate the paper specification, observed code and our proposed corrections. Water's [Stage 2 guide](../water/docs/PAPER_STUDY_GUIDE.md) demonstrates source study and mapping, not execution. Preserve original bytes, link reproducibility questions and leave human-reading status unconfirmed until the researcher supplies a scope-specific account.

Check when residuals are formed, when updates are committed and which history is admitted. ## Additional manufacturing procedure: open data and a shared-paper shortlist

Status: **source and static-code audit executed; data loading/experiments not validated**. Evidence: manufacturing [selection report](../manufacturing/docs/BASELINE_SELECTION_REPORT.md) and MFG-004 in its [log](../manufacturing/docs/WORK_LOG.md).

1. Start with actual sector provenance and attack/normal/fault semantics. Screen public providers and recent papers together; avoid choosing a familiar industrial dataset whose physical process belongs to another sector.
2. Check provider metadata, version, license and file inventory. For large archives, inspect a bounded HTTP response without reading the body; record GET versus HEAD and authentication state. A failed HEAD can coexist with a working GET. Preserve only safe URLs, never transient signed download tokens.
3. Build the intersection using paper citations **and actual loader paths**. Same names, sizes, simulator or repository family do not establish identical data. Separate original and successor collections, corrected files, and code/data licenses.
4. Trace labels and input availability as carefully as model architecture. Synthetic outlier labels, random-row sampling, incompatible modalities stacked as rows and pre-split fitting can change the task or invalidate our protocol. Static code observations do not establish which revision produced a publication's results.
5. Record a learning set separately from execution admission. Do not count three classifiers in one paper as three papers, fault-only scores as cyberattack results, or cloud state storage as demonstrated physical prediction. Save missing full texts, permissions and release/run mappings as blockers.
6. Before reuse in another sector, independently verify that sector's data, threats and observation assumptions. Public data solves access, not experimental validity or novelty. Update the active project only; do not copy its completion state to siblings.

Expected artifacts: admission criteria, selection decision, dated provider/manuscript/code screens, project status/log, and a proposed next acquisition/protocol task. Checks: local source links, provenance/revision consistency, raw-data inventory, no unintended installs/execution or sibling changes. ## Further lessons from manufacturing receipt and Step 0

- A file called `fixed` may only change serialization. Compare original and corrected files positionally under a stated numeric representation; preserve both byte hashes and the canonicalization specification. Equal binary64 values do not establish identical decimal source text, new labels, corrected clocks or independent recordings. Manufacturing's [identity audit](../manufacturing/docs/CASPER_DATASET_IDENTITY.md) demonstrates this check.
- Distinguish nominal sampling rates from verified clock units and mapping. Preserve native timestamps, offsets, gaps, and missing time fields. Never transfer labels by equal row index across asynchronous devices or infer event delay from a guessed samples-per-second factor.
- Trace the whole preprocessing/predictor/score-aggregation support. Consecutive windows increase effective history and delay; purging only the largest individual window can be insufficient. Keep score support, target convention, unknown gaps and event coverage explicit in the protocol.
- In a beginner lesson, connect physical state, prediction, residual, score, threshold and evaluation before architecture. Clarify that the deployed detector does not receive true attack labels; only evaluation requires ground truth. Separate normal calibration from training and distinguish precision's alarm denominator from a false-positive rate's normal-data denominator. Manufacturing's [lesson](../manufacturing/docs/RESEARCH_BASICS_LESSON.md) is prepared and arithmetic-checked; [researcher understanding](../manufacturing/docs/YOUR_STEP_0_NOTES.md) remains unassessed.

## Deeper source and data study after receipt

Procedure audited in manufacturing's [data/material study](../manufacturing/docs/DATA_AND_BASELINE_STUDY.md); benchmark execution remains unstarted. Prerequisites: an authorized immutable dataset receipt, matched source revisions, sufficient local space and a bounded reading/audit scope.

1. Inventory existing bytes before downloading. Extend a receipt with a separate audit manifest; retain original raw hashes and executed-script provenance. Expand a bounded schema sample to a complete nested-value scan when feature parsing matters. Numeric validity does not establish physical authenticity.
2. Fetch complete repository-tree metadata at a fixed commit, then collect relevant source/configuration/license material. Validate Git blob identity and SHA-256, and separate checkpoints, datasets, scripts and reported result artifacts. Inspect scripts without importing them: top-level imports can trigger experiments.
3. Trace every index space: startup removal, trimming, causal filtering, prediction windows, score aggregation and target assignment. Record endpoint and support separately. Verify the union of consecutive histories with a small synthetic index check. Never interpret that software check as measured detection performance.
4. Compare paper figures, prose, tables and code explicitly. Figure architecture can resolve incomplete prose; confusion-matrix class totals can test index bookkeeping. Neither establishes reproduced predictions or exact published training data. Preserve conflicting row counts and distinguish unverified from false claims.
5. Resolve field semantics through matched acquisition code and primary device documentation. State when a later source is only supporting evidence for an older dataset. An elapsed-time unit does not prove shared wall-clock synchronization; scheduled injection flags are not automatically measured physical-onset labels.
6. Keep author tables/graphs in ignored baseline-source storage, marked unreplicated. Maintain a beginner reading order and an evidence-to-code map. Missing manuscripts stay open issues; source availability does not establish three fair experimental baselines.

For a dedicated paper-to-code pass, record main-text versus abstract-only coverage, exact section/figure/cell/function anchors, and components described in the paper but absent from the inspected repository. Trace saved result columns back to source metric calls and raw author run summaries: headings can mislabel validation/test, weighted F1 can differ from positive-class F1, SD conventions can change between summaries, and seconds per batch cannot be ranked as seconds per observation. Arithmetic consistency of author summaries is a provenance check, not a reproduction or proof of valid experimental design. Manufacturing's [reading map](../manufacturing/docs/PAPER_CODE_TRACEABILITY.md) records this applied procedure; the later [admission audit](../manufacturing/docs/COMPARATOR_ADMISSION.md) obtained TWINE, leaving CatBoost full text inaccessible.

Expected artifacts: separate source/result manifest, full-schema report, annotation/index study, accessible reading guide, updated project status/log, and unresolved execution gates. Checks: exact hashes, syntax/structural parsing, source correspondence, links, raw immutability and ignore coverage. Before repeating in another sector, independently validate its units, labels, input availability and data permissions; do not copy manufacturing's conclusions.

## Consolidating release, label and clock identity

Status: **executed/validated for received manufacturing CASPER files** within the [identity register](../manufacturing/docs/DATASET_IDENTITY_REGISTER.md); no experimental split or model is validated. Prerequisites: an authorized unchanged receipt, available raw bytes, prior schema evidence and a bounded passive audit scope.

1. Distinguish provider revision, received bytes and published-run identity. Recheck provider metadata separately from fresh full-file fingerprints. Preserve the original receipt; create a separate recheck and a consolidated manifest with evidence hashes. File names and sizes alone do not prove byte equality across successor listings or published experiments.
2. Preserve serialized timestamp strings at endpoints and annotation transitions. Record zero-based half-open indices, previous/first-positive/last-positive/next records and the exact event convention. Decimal arithmetic preserves serialization; it does not add clock precision. Keep detailed observation metadata ignored when only summary provenance belongs in Git.
3. Trace which physical device receives a changed command and when flags are attached relative to state receipt and command dispatch. A flag copied to two streams does not establish two manipulated devices, physical-effect onset or synchronized samples. Retain uncertainty when supporting capture code postdates the dataset.
4. Inspect container magic and timestamp options instead of trusting suffixes. For capture files, review per-section/per-interface resolution/offset, missing timestamps and adjacent duplicate/backward steps while leaving packets unchanged. Decode only the headers needed for the task; validate parser assumptions with explicitly synthetic fixtures. Valid framing and retained lengths do not establish lossless acquisition or clock accuracy.
5. Separate release date, controller elapsed time, capture epoch time and untimestamped row order. Missing time or labels are observed absences; do not fill them from nominal rates or neighboring modalities. Record what a defensible delay/false-alarm metric would still require.

Expected artifacts: identity register, machine-readable manifest, standard checksum file with an explicit working directory, label/clock report, paper-specific correspondence and updated project log/status. Checks: receipt unchanged, all acquired-file hashes match, metadata/source hashes resolve, raw storage stays ignored/read-only, parser fixtures are marked synthetic, and annotation/split admission remains separate. A consolidated manifest should distinguish literal command output from later source-review supplements. Reuse the procedure, not the manufacturing findings, in the other projects.

## Selecting a testable question and freezing the data contract

Status: **design procedure applied and metadata checked in manufacturing; model/benchmark validation not executed**. Evidence: the [selected question/threat model](../manufacturing/docs/RESEARCH_QUESTION_AND_THREAT_MODEL.md), [contract](../manufacturing/docs/ANNOTATION_FEATURE_SPLIT_CONTRACT.md) and [critique](../manufacturing/docs/RQ_CONTRACT_REVIEW.md).

1. Choose a question from observations the acquired data can actually support. List missing comparison cases such as legitimate changeovers, faults or independent runs. If changing modality gives a better state/time representation, disclose how that changes the attacker/trust model and baseline designation. Do not force three learning papers into an unfair numerical ranking.
2. Separate the demonstrated intervention from the proposed security scenario. Define the attacker's command/measurement/model access and which path remains truthful. A telemetry field or acquisition script does not establish authentication, physical-effect onset or malicious intent.
3. Specify what the twin represents, predicts and synchronizes. Keep nominal references separate from observed quantities that an attacker can change; otherwise state tracking may absorb the target deviation. Include simple controls that directly measure the manipulated variable and ablations that isolate the proposed mechanism. Failure to beat simple controls is a reportable result.
4. Record exact source hash, annotation version, feature allowlist, native-time use and chronological half-open ranges before model scoring. Preserve labels outside the detector interface. Split before transformations/windows, distinguish fitted parameters from dynamic state and specify gap/warmup handling. Run final replay continuously through attacks; labels never trigger resets.
5. Report alarm behavior honestly at boundaries: an existing alarm overlapping an event is not a new alarm onset; a positive first eligible record can have a censored onset. Long event windows can make eventual hit recall misleading, so pair it with point-level behavior, normal alarm burden and coverage. Use supported time bases and explicit exposure denominators.
6. Review the contract structurally, then keep model-specific initialization, finite normal-only selection rules, scoring and executable loader/metric tests as separate gates. A metadata validator cannot prove a future implementation is leak-free. Save contrary reviewer recommendations and justify the choice from evidence; an internal review is not external peer review or novelty proof.

Expected artifacts: selected RQ/threat-model decision, versioned annotation/features/splits, source-check note, independent design critique, status/log updates and validation receipt. Keep method selection independent of final attack outcomes; retain absent evidence and publication limitations. These are reusable steps, not a requirement for other sectors to adopt manufacturing's selected inputs or assumptions.

## Defining calibration, metrics and a protected final campaign

Status: **specified and metadata/arithmetic reviewed in manufacturing; executable enforcement and experiments remain unstarted**. Evidence: [evaluation plan](../manufacturing/docs/EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md), [metric definitions](../manufacturing/docs/METRIC_DEFINITIONS.md), [final-test protection](../manufacturing/docs/FINAL_TEST_PROTECTION.md). Prerequisites: a selected feasible question, explicit trust boundary, immutable data identity and a reviewed annotation/feature/split contract.

1. Separate fitting, normal-only model selection, threshold calibration and final evaluation. Publish exact half-open ranges, support masks and exclusion counts. Protect the final normal block as strongly as the intervention block; zero labels do not permit reuse for tuning. Continuous final state need not reset at a reporting boundary.
2. Freeze model choices before calibration. Define quantile rank, comparison operator, tie handling, numeric provenance and failure cases explicitly. Fit each frozen model/seed's threshold on the same permitted calibration population. An empirical upper-tail fraction is not a future false-alarm guarantee. Do not rank models on calibration behavior or refit afterward while keeping old thresholds.
3. Specify counts and denominators before aggregate metrics. Distinguish positive-class F1, noninterpolated average precision, normal point alarms, observed alarm episodes, native-time exposure and event overlap versus fresh onset. Mark unknown/censored boundaries and missing delays; do not confuse schedule-relative or processing delay with physical attack latency. Synthetic examples establish expected arithmetic, not benchmark performance.
4. Predefine coverage admission. Unexpected missing/nonfinite scores must not improve performance by removing difficult endpoints or shrinking the common mask. Retain every declared failure. Structural warmup/gap exclusions and model failures are different records.
5. Declare seeds, paired contrasts, complete run matrices and descriptive summaries before outcomes. Do not pick a best seed, pool repeated trajectories as new observations or treat one recording's rows/events/seeds as independent plant experiments. Mandatory controls and negative findings stay in the report.
