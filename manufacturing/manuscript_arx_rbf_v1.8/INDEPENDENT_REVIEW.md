## Findings

### Scientific reporting passes its stated limits

The abstract correctly reports the initial 17-control/189-episode RBF audit, the separate 11-method/216-episode contextual campaign, the exposed RAPT result, and the prospective normal-only 33-method pilot. It explicitly states that the candidate fails and that originality, superiority and real-robot recovery are not established. The conclusion retains the earlier failed criteria rather than presenting the latest normal RMSE improvement as the intended contribution.

The selected adapter/direct-reference values match the source record: RMSE 0.103372809 versus 0.116228148, an 11.06% reduction; radius proxy 3.695331727 versus 3.409818803, an 8.37% worsening. The same-budget FIR RMSE of 0.087016931 makes the second relevant comparison fail. The primary radius condition and same-budget RMSE condition remain failed while execution and other stated clauses pass. No fresh attack campaign is represented as executed or justified by this pilot.

The manuscript identifies the radius as an empirical development-selection proxy, not an independently calibrated or physical guarantee. It preserves single-plant/single-neural-seed limitations, exposed normal populations, fixed epoch selection, window versus full-episode initialization, and the difference between real-arithmetic bounds and verified machine arithmetic. Its clipping comparison is descriptive after the gate: the C=0.25 clipped FIR has smaller reported RMSE and radius than all six constrained candidates, and every constrained candidate's tighter proxy is cap-derived. These statements agree with the retained table/audit and do not replace the original criterion.

The separate NumPy replay's stated maximum difference, 2.39e-7, safely rounds the saved 2.384185791015625e-7 maximum. Exact PyTorch reload and numerical NumPy agreement are correctly distinguished. Resource figures use decimal MB where written and do not become isolated inference latency claims.

### RAPT and recovery interpretation remain scoped

The 9/24 detections, 15 misses, 5,175/70,800 benign alarms, 9,448/10,689 physically outside inactive points and six persistent-offset late-quarter misses agree with the retained exploratory report. The 518,400 exactly reproduced channel scores and 129,600/126,000 total/eligible row counts agree with the no-fit decomposition report. Mean/variance notation now separates predicted log variance from velocity channel v1.

The decomposition supports heterogeneous task/channel changes, not a universal collapse of position error or variance-inflation-only account. The manuscript does not attribute causality to recurrence or claim that fixed network parameters learned an attack online. It preserves the transferred detector-core, exposed-data and unequal-training restrictions. The physical-return definitions remain evaluator-only and finite; neither inactivity at return nor a falling edge is described as permanent recovery.

### Prior-art/version treatment is accurate

The added text separates iResNet residual-map separation, Kim's Lipschitz-autoencoder anomaly separation and DiffInt's support-conditional pointwise bound. The candidate's reverse-triangle calculation is expressly not a new theorem. Gualandi supports protected prediction as prior art, without suggesting that a bare KF reproduces the entire published method.

The sufficient-sensitivity future-work statement is attributed only to Ghanipoor's inspected 2025 v1.
