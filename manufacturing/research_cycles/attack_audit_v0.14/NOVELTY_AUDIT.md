# Candidate novelty and comparator audit

17 October 2026. Target: the exact v0.13 candidate, not digital twins in general. This is a focused primary-source review, not a systematic search or an assurance that all prior work has been found. Earlier study results are preserved; a new experiment cannot make a familiar component original.

## Claim-by-claim assessment before outcomes

| Candidate claim | Evidence and reasoning | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Learn a correction to a robot model | Yang et al. combine an EKF with a residual neural network for manipulator calibration; Chen et al.'s 2025 survey covers data-driven robot calibration and cites RBF compensation. The candidate uses a different, simpler RBF output correction, but the broad model-plus-learned-correction idea already exists. | Do not claim residual correction itself as new. Full equation-level comparison with additional original correction methods remains necessary for a distinct algorithm claim. |
| Prevent measured attacks from changing the reference | Inspection shows the reference is a deterministic function of frozen coefficients, approved commands and clean initialization. Later measurements have no argument/path into it. This is a structural consequence of using an open-loop reference, verified by perturbation tests. | Useful property under the stated trust boundary; not evidence that we invented attack-resistant adaptation. The candidate does not adapt online. |
| Short and long return evidence | The exact rule is the intersection of two moving-mean threshold tests followed by a consecutive-success counter, with alarm entry priority. | A transparent hysteresis/persistence construction. No distinct mathematical ingredient is demonstrated. Its cost/benefit must be measured against single-window and no-dwell controls. |
| Normal-only detection | FAIL-Detect learns/scores from successful robot demonstrations and calibrates a sequential threshold without failure training data. CASPER/Edge predict expected IMU behavior. | Neither normal-only training nor robotic prediction-error detection is new. Different input/task assumptions prevent copying their headline scores into our table. |
| Counterfactual recovery evaluation | RESCUE evaluates VLA runtime alarms through counterfactual interventions. Our evaluator compares a disturbed plant to an uncorrupted same-plant trajectory; the target is different. | State the target difference precisely. Do not claim the invention of counterfactual robot evaluation. |
| Better attack detection | Not known before the v0.14 run. v0.13's normal-reference improvement gate failed. | Test the unchanged candidate with matched controls; preserve failure and negative results. A secondary win is not overall superiority. |

Current novelty verdict: **not established**. It would be misleading to invent a method name and label this assembly a new architecture. A worthwhile measured difference could motivate further work, but an engineering benefit and algorithmic originality are separate requirements.

## Comparator admission

The v0.14 table contains 17 independent implementations of controls/ablations under common command/state access, not 17 paper reproductions. Existing CASPER/Edge CNNs remain a separate IMU track. FAIL-Detect's complete policy inputs/learned density score are absent here, so a generic threshold is not labelled FAIL-Detect. Classens et al.'s structured identification work remains background; ordinary ARX is not its algorithm. Authors' code-use restrictions and missing manuscript details have not been silently waived.

## Newly located independent-evidence lead

[FactoryNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09081v5) separates setpoints, effort, feedback and context, making it a potentially better fit for command-conditioned references than IMU-only CASPER. The verified arXiv history is v1: 9 May 2026 and v5: 8 October 2026. The current abstract lists acceptance at the AI4Physics and FMSD **workshops** at ICML 2026; this is not verified acceptance to the ICML main proceedings. The [author repository](https://github.com/Forgis-Labs/factorynet) has an older-looking author/count description and a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 notice; exact paper/data/version/license correspondence still requires audit.

The current abstract reports limitations of cross-manufacturer transfer and uncalibrated simulator fault signals, which directly cautions against using this small simulation as real-robot confirmation. This is a lead only: no observation downloaded, no dataset admitted, no cybersecurity labels or trusted commands assumed. OpenReview's PDF endpoint presented a browser challenge; arXiv was used as a lawful public manuscript route instead. Version 5 must govern further reading rather than silently mixing it with the repository's older claims.
