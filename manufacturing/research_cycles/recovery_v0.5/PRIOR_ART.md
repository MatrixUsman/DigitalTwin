## Algebraic check before simulation

Let `zf` be normalized following error and `zr` normalized command-reference error. The proposed trigger `zf>h OR zr>h` is exactly `max(zf,zr)>h`. Its recovery condition `zf<=l AND zr<=l` is exactly `max(zf,zr)<=l`. With identical validity handling and dwell count, both algorithms produce the same state, count and alarm at every step, by induction from the common initial state.

Therefore **this candidate is an implementation of fused-error dwell-time hysteresis**. The two independently written functions are required to agree in random synthetic tests and every pilot episode. It is useful as a mechanism/control experiment but does not pass our original-algorithm admission gate. Renaming the two observers or reporting an improvement over a point threshold would not change this conclusion.

## What the pilot can establish

The question is whether a protected command-driven reference supplies sustained-deviation information that a corrected local observer loses, and what alarm burden/recovery behavior follows under explicit dynamics. Controls isolate context (AR versus ARX), immutable reference, temporal averaging, accumulation and hysteresis. Authorized/unauthorized offset episodes share identical physical measurements: the difference in intent is supplied by trusted context, not inferred magically from motion.

Trusted planner commands, their validity indication and truthful joint-state sensing are assumed. A compromised planner or jointly forged state/context can invalidate the interpretation. Parameter mismatch is tested at two declared levels; no physical safety, general fault/attack distinction or robust bounds follow.

**Contribution decision before execution:** do not advance this exact rule as the novel method. Execute the bounded pilot to obtain reusable physics, information-access and recovery evidence. Any next candidate must specify a non-equivalent operation and compare it with this exact classical control and the closest accessible recovery methods.
