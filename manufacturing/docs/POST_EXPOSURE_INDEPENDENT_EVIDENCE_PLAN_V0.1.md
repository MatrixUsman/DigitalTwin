# Independent evidence after the CASPER outcome

**Version/date:** v0.1, 17 October 2026. **Status:** prospective plan written after final CASPER exposure. **No new dataset acquired/admitted, no new observations opened, no training or redesign executed.** This supplements the historical [pre-exposure plan](INDEPENDENT_EVIDENCE_PLAN.md) without rewriting it. The [final results](FINAL_CAMPAIGN_REPORT_V0.1.md) and [plain-language interpretation](FINDINGS_EXPLAINED_V0.1.md) explain why the next study needs a separate evidence record.

## Decision now

Retain the completed paper as a narrow score-comparison case study. Do not upgrade its claim to a validated digital twin or general cyberattack detector. Begin the next scientific milestone with a bounded **voraus-AD metadata and compatibility admission audit**, followed by acquisition only under a concrete separately authorized scope. Its possible claim is portability of normal-trained robotic motion monitoring to independent physical-anomaly executions. NIST is a complementary condition-change/false-alarm route. A cyber-intent claim needs different evidence.

These routes have different roles:

| Route | Proposed question | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Existing CASPER | Explain the frozen score comparison; separately labelled exploration if later requested | Fresh confirmation of any outcome-driven redesign |
| Original voraus-AD | Can a motion-only procedure remain meaningful across variable executions on another robot? | Malicious intent or cross-robot cyberattack detection from physical-fault labels |
| NIST UR5 condition recordings | How does the monitor behave under documented benign speed/load/temperature conditions? | Attack sensitivity; within-run changeover robustness unless actual transitions are verified |
| New authorized laboratory recordings | Can trusted context distinguish matched authorized changes from command-path interventions across sessions? | Any result before the facility, authority, ground truth and recordings exist |

The [voraus-AD author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04765) and [pinned author repository](https://github.com/vorausrobotik/voraus-ad-dataset/tree/a91a86a642d23df58833b792a53de01edfd81abe) describe joint-state data from variable executions and physical anomalies. The [NIST provider](https://www.nist.gov/el/intelligent-systems-division-73500/degradation-measurement-robot-arm-position-accuracy) lists controller recordings at documented operating settings. These are source descriptions, not locally verified files or new experimental results. Detailed versions/limits remain in the [existing source screen](literature/INDEPENDENT_EVIDENCE_SCREEN_2026-10-17.md). A current browser refetch of the pinned voraus repository failed; the earlier saved author metadata/code receipt remains available. Reverify access before acquisition.

## Stage A: admit one source and one claim

1. Record the exact intended claim and the role of each information channel. Start with actual q/qdot and elapsed time. Additional effort, command or program-node inputs require a separately declared information track.
2. Recheck original provider, exact release, access, data terms and code terms. Prior metadata describes 100 Hz and 500 Hz voraus variants; choose one before downloading, based on native-time needs and local storage. Prefer the smaller 100 Hz version for an initial feasibility audit if its original samples and field semantics support the question. This is a proposal, not a validated release decision.
3. Obtain only a provider manifest/schema/documentation initially where possible. Record which files encode sessions, executions, nominal variants and anomaly families. Verify whether identifiers imply chronology; never infer that from sorting filenames.
4. On authorized receipt, preserve original bytes and record archive/member sizes and SHA-256. Establish robot, task, acquisition/session/day and execution lineage. Detect duplicate/reprocessed source families by metadata and hashes. Repackaged voraus observations in another corpus are not a second independent acquisition.
5. Inspect units, joint order, actual versus commanded values, native timestamps, gaps, row rates and start/end conventions. Record annotation mechanism, timing uncertainty and physical-anomaly semantics. Do not change fault labels into attack labels.
6. Record a pass/hold decision for q/qdot applicability. Short or branching executions may invalidate a single circular phase reference. Lack of appropriate periodic structure is a scientific reason to change the question or decline the source, not permission to force an artificial period.

**Output before implementation:** source receipt, independent-group register, source-specific input/threat contract, ground-truth limitations, estimated storage, and a named pass/hold decision. No actual train/calibration/final group assignment can be finalized until group lineage exists. No additional dataset is currently admitted.

## Stage B: define fresh separation before model work

Allocate complete acquisition/session groups where possible, then whole executions within the appropriate groups. Keep all windows and repeats from a held-out group together. If acquisition chronology is unavailable, describe the design as grouped held-out execution evaluation, not chronological validation. Record condition/task-family membership and duplicate handling before making windows.

Maintain four distinct uses: normal fitting; normal development for the finite search and correspondence checks; normal threshold-only calibration; protected final normal plus physical-anomaly recordings. Do not inspect protected final features merely because their labels are hidden. Metadata needed to allocate groups must be logged separately from performance/outcome inspection. A feasible allocation depends on available independent groups, not arbitrary row percentages.

Separate the claims:

- A new scaler/reference fitted per source tests applicability of the procedure.
- Reserved conditions or programs test a specified within-platform transfer question.
- Unchanged cross-robot deployment without refitting is a stronger zero-shot question and requires a defensible joint/coordinate mapping. It is not the default.

The current CASPER results may inform explicit exploratory design choices, but they cannot be relabelled as validation of those choices. If a new source is used for exploration and outcomes are inspected, reserve an additional independent set for confirmation. Record all exposure and keep the old comparison visible.

## Stage C: specify a bounded normal-only feasibility study

Before fitting, define reset rules in native time, startup history, permitted lookback and minimum coverage. Do not copy the 38-second CASPER period or 1,528-row warmup. At 100 Hz the latter would span 15.28 seconds, which may exceed an entire short execution; per-source duration metadata must determine feasibility. Do not concatenate separate executions into a fictitious continuous cycle, pad endpoints as normal observations, or discard difficult runs after scoring.

Declare a finite normal-only configuration set and deterministic tie rules. The minimal explanatory roster retains velocity deviation, ordinary causal prediction, an appropriate fixed reference, and a matched residual-versus-correction comparison only if the new observer has defensible state correspondence. A modern learned or causal alignment comparator is needed before reviving broad detector/estimator superiority claims; admission includes inputs, chronology, selection, code license and resource feasibility. No method is mandatory merely to fill a three-paper quota.

Synthetic loader, group-isolation, score-alignment, reset and metric fixtures must pass before a one-attempt normal-only pilot. Set wall/CPU/memory/output caps from the source's actual size and a concrete command; do not silently inherit or expand CASPER's budgets. Preserve every failure. No lengthy training, paid computation or robot operation is authorized by this plan.

**Stop or revise before new final exposure if:** group independence is unresolved, normal-only calibration cannot be separated, trajectories lack the assumed state correspondence, warmup destroys coverage, or a simpler method explains the normal prediction equally well. A revised model at this point needs a new recorded design/version. It is not a change to the completed CASPER experiment.

## Stage D: predeclare what would count as useful evidence

The primary scientific contrast can remain the added correction term with identical state trajectories, if that is still the question. Report its sign, absolute magnitude and normal alarm costs. The numerical benefit needed for an application cannot be invented from CASPER's final differences. Before new final exposure, specify an operator-relevant alarm budget and response horizon with domain input, or explicitly retain a descriptive case-study aim without a deployment claim.

A 99th-percentile normal threshold can be retained as a declared methodological comparison, but must not be described as an operational guarantee. Any episode aggregation, persistence or cooldown is a new design choice: specify it on normal development and freeze it, never choose it from final fault/attack outcomes. Threshold-only calibration follows model selection.

Report every method and every eligible run: unadjusted point metrics and AP, normal point/episode burden per native-time exposure, run/event coverage, fresh onsets versus overlaps, explicit misses and schedule/annotation-relative delays. Include per-condition outcomes without outcome-selected subgroups. Pre-instrument model-only processing, initialization, parsing, resource use and end-to-end timing separately if cost comparisons are a claim. The completed CASPER campaign cannot supply those missing method-level timings.

Rows, overlapping windows and seeds remain dependent. Define the independent sampling unit from the acquisition process. Any interval estimation or power calculation must use defensible run/session dependence and a meaningful effect selected before outcomes; no universal number of recordings guarantees adequacy. More executions on one testbed still do not represent a population of robots.

## Stage E: fresh confirmation and possible cyber study

Freeze the complete model/seed roster, sources, group splits, code, artifacts, thresholds, metrics and budgets before protected execution. Seal every score stream before delayed labels and joint release. Preserve all failures, duplicates excluded by prior rules, and null/negative findings. The conclusion must name the evidence track: physical anomalies, benign variation or actual documented cyber interventions.

If the intended contribution is specifically fault/attack/authorized-change discrimination, obtain a separately authorized testbed or compatible original recordings with matched actions and trusted contextual logs. Include repeated sessions across days and task/condition variations, with a reserved set. A documented speed change alone does not reveal who authorized it. If no independent trusted information distinguishes the cases, narrow the detection target instead of promising intent inference. No facility, budget, robot access, human-subject approval or cyber experiment is assumed to exist here.

## Deliverable and decision after this plan

The next executable task is a **source-specific admission audit**, not a model training command. Its outcome must be one of: (a) compatible independent physical-anomaly study with a defined new contract; (b) a different explicitly scoped question; or (c) no suitable next source. Keep the current manuscript's weak findings unchanged in every case. No new dataset, result, title claim or publication approval follows from this plan alone.
