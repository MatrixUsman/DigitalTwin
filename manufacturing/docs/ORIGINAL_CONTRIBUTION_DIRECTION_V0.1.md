## Questions worth investigating, not novelty claims

The preferred next investigation is whether a predictive motion model can remain synchronized with **permitted** execution variation while retaining evidence of **unauthorized** motion changes. This is a candidate problem, not a selected architecture. Two specific possibilities must be checked against prior art and available evidence:

1. **Synchronization without concealing deviations.** Under a declared sensor/trust model, can a model separate plausible execution progression from changes that invalidate its nominal motion prediction? Before designing a guard, demonstrate that a specified baseline actually loses useful evidence through synchronization. The current R-versus-RC experiment does not demonstrate this for a paper baseline, and adding phase/correction surprise is already covered by close prior work. Retain ordinary velocity, forecasting, alignment and innovation-monitoring controls.
2. **Authorized operating context versus anomalous motion.** Can trusted task/command context improve monitoring across legitimate speed or load changes without accepting unauthorized interventions? This needs independently justified trusted context and documented benign as well as malicious changes. Giving only our method extra context requires an explicit information-access study, not a claim of architectural superiority. Without such context, identical observations cannot establish different intentions.

Both routes are relevant to the user's field. Neither currently passes novelty or evidence-feasibility admission. The original CASPER collection alone does not establish the required authorized-change comparison. Purely synthetic changes can support labelled mechanism tests, but cannot replace real or validated testbed evidence for physical cybersecurity claims.

The [periodic-alignment](literature/PERIODIC_ALIGNMENT_PRIOR_ART_2026-10-17.md) and [innovation/synchronization](literature/INNOVATION_SYNCHRONIZATION_PRIOR_ART_2026-10-17.md) audits remain constraints. Include task-segmented CASPER successor methods and other close approaches in the new search; do not compare only against the weakest inspected workflow. No exhaustive novelty search has been completed for either question.
