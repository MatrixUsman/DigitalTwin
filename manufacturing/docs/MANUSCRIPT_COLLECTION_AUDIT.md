## CASPER: input, labels, model and decision

Assistant reading covered the title/version page, Sections 6.3–6.4, 6.6 and 7, Table 10, Table 11, Table 14 and Algorithm 1. Relevant pages were text-extracted; pages 1, 21 and 24 were also rendered and inspected. This is a targeted methods reading, not complete reference verification or a claim that the authors have read the paper.

The physical experiment changes robotic-arm joint velocity during a pick-and-place task. The detector in this paper uses the external Nicla IMU. Controller telemetry and network recordings are additional dataset modalities, not demonstrated inputs to the original CASPER detector. Its security rationale is observing physical effects independently of potentially compromised network measurements. A motion anomaly does not alone identify malicious intent.

Section 6.6 explicitly describes cause-independent detection: the physical deviation could arise from an attack, degradation, power failure or an accident. It also acknowledges training-time compromise as a vulnerability while arguing that staff would likely notice deviations. That is an assumption about clean commissioning and human oversight, not evidence that training contamination cannot happen. A future research question must specify trusted sensors and training conditions rather than inherit that assumption silently.

For timing, distinguish controller schedule, observed clock, row index, filtered-row index and the end of a score's support. A universal conversion `row / 20` is not justified by this audit. The 755-sample predictor and subsequent 755-residual aggregation also introduce a warm-up/support interval; classifier runtime is not end-to-end attack-detection delay. Until cross-clock alignment and boundary uncertainty are documented, precise event delays and comparisons across modalities remain unsupported.

Two specific documentation inconsistencies should not be propagated: the notebook's last manual interval ends at 1,716,450 in executable source but at 1,716,456 in its explanatory text; and the corrected manuscript's Section 6.4.3 repeats the false-positive description for false negatives. Page 21 was visually checked to rule out a text-extraction artifact. Our metrics must use the standard definition: a false negative is an actual anomaly predicted normal. These findings do not establish that every published numerical result is wrong.

## Consequences for the proposed common comparison

The collection now provides a concrete CASPER reading starter and pinned static evidence. It does not make the trio execution-ready. CASPER uses external IMU with normal-only fitting; TWINE uses controller telemetry with attack labels during fitting; the CatBoost script combines two target definitions. Those differences require separate information-access tracks or explicitly described reimplementations under common inputs, labels and chronological partitions. Even an open dataset does not resolve them.

Before training: freeze actual file hashes and derivative lineage; retain authentic annotation provenance; establish timestamps/episode boundaries; settle fair input and training-label access; choose calibration without held-out attacks; and resolve the missing manuscripts or keep those candidates on hold. Compare the detected physical deviations supported by the data, without converting them into proof of universal cyberattack attribution or live deployment.
