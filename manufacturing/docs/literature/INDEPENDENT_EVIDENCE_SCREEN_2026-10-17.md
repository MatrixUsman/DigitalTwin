# Independent evidence beyond the original CASPER acquisition

Manufacturing / Haiqa Mushtaq. Audit: **17 October 2026 (Asia/Karachi)**. Scope: primary-source documentation, release metadata, manuscripts and static loader inspection using the shared academic-research skill's bounded literature/source-verification workflow. **No new dataset observations, archive, fitted model or experiment were opened or executed. No additional dataset is admitted.** Public metadata/documents were preserved under ignored `local/independent_evidence_20261017/`; calibration and final CASPER observations stayed closed.

## Decision

The current evidence supports an **empirical, single-acquisition feasibility study of synchronization evidence in repetitive robot motion**. It does not support general cyberattack detection across robots, tasks, operational changeovers or faults. Eight scheduled intervals in one acquisition are not eight independent robot experiments. Additional rows, windows, channels, resampling variants or folds from that acquisition do not create external replication. See the existing [identity register](../DATASET_IDENTITY_REGISTER.md) and [observer validation](../OBSERVER_VALIDATION_REPORT.md).

The best next expansion to audit is **the original voraus-AD release**, as a separate normal-operation/physical-anomaly track. It supplies an independently collected robot/application with compatible joint-state channels and variable nominal motions. It does **not** supply cyberattack ground truth. NIST's UR5 condition-change recordings are a useful subsequent benign-condition challenge. Neither establishes attack intent, and neither can inherit the CASPER protocol unchanged.

If the intended claim remains specifically about distinguishing malicious speed changes from legitimate changes, public fault data are insufficient. That claim needs independently documented authorized and unauthorized command changes, trusted command/task context, and repeated acquisition sessions—or must be narrowed. The metadata routes below are recommendations for a separately scoped acquisition/admission audit, not automatic download, training or test exposure.

## CASPER 1 & 2 is not an automatic independent replication

Fresh [provider metadata](https://www.kaggle.com/api/v1/datasets/view/hkayan/industrial-robotic-arm-imu-data-casper-1-and-2) still identifies v2, last updated `2024-09-01T09:55:44.95Z`. The description concerns a UR3e performing screwdriving, painting and pick-and-place, with impact, weight, magnetic and vibration disturbances. The [file inventory](https://www.kaggle.com/api/v1/datasets/list/hkayan/industrial-robotic-arm-imu-data-casper-1-and-2) lists `right_arm.csv` at 1,978,193,094 bytes and `nicla.csv` at 138,908,648 bytes—the same advertised sizes as our original members. Equal size is **not** a measured byte match. No successor raw bytes were acquired in this screen.

Five filenames identify disturbance material: `IMU_earthquake.csv`, `IMU_extra_weigth.csv`, `IMU_hitting_arm.csv`, `IMU_hitting_platform.csv`, and `IMU_magnet.csv`. The other files include multiple IMU sampling/representation variants. Their independent acquisition dates, controller-state correspondence and exact temporal annotation are unresolved. The [author repository](https://github.com/hkayann/Real-time-Anomaly-Detection-in-Industrial-Robotic-Arms-via-TinyML) describes external disturbances, not five independently verified cyberattacks.

Before any reuse, establish original-versus-derived lineage, hashes, experiment IDs, sensor modality, clocks and label meaning. A frequency variant, quaternion conversion or crop cannot be treated as an independent test recording. Original CC BY-SA terms remain attached to our original receipt; successor MIT listing metadata does not silently change those terms.

## Priority route: voraus-AD

The [manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.04765v1), Sections III-A–F and Table II, describes **1,367 nominal and 755 anomalous executions** on a six-axis Yu-Cobot. Joint position, joint velocity and time in seconds are explicit fields. Random object placement creates legitimate trajectory/timing variation. Twelve physical-anomaly types include gripping problems, collision, friction and deliberately altered commutation; these labels do not establish attacker intent. The average execution is approximately 11 seconds. Some faults may principally affect effort/torque while q/qdot barely change, so our twelve-channel restriction can limit detectability. This is a meaningful challenge to motion-only claims, not a reason to silently add privileged channels.

The [author repository at the inspected revision](https://github.com/vorausrobotik/voraus-ad-dataset/tree/a91a86a642d23df58833b792a53de01edfd81abe) provides 100 Hz and 500 Hz downloads, approximately 1.1 GB and 5.3 GB respectively, and distinguishes **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 data from MIT code**. These are advertised sizes, not locally measured payloads. No download response or raw-file checksum was checked here. The pinned loader's `load_pandas_dataframes` chooses `PRE_A` executions for training, other variants for test, fits scaling on training, and optionally pads/truncates executions. That is an inspected upstream protocol, not our admitted protocol. Padding, timing, chronological order and any unseen-run validation split need separate audit. [Pinned loader](https://github.com/vorausrobotik/voraus-ad-dataset/blob/a91a86a642d23df58833b792a53de01edfd81abe/voraus_ad.py)

Publication: arXiv v1 was submitted 2023-11-08. The [author institution](https://research.uni-hannover.de/en/publications/the-voraus-ad-dataset-for-anomaly-detection-in-robot-applications/) reports online publication 2023-11-13; Crossref identifies IEEE Transactions on Robotics, volume 40, print year 2024, pages 438–451. Publisher DOI access failed in this browser; Crossref, author manuscript and institutional record support the journal identity. The date distinction is retained rather than forcing a single year. No author performance number is imported as ours.

**Why it helps:** separate acquisition, different arm, variable executions and explicit physical-anomaly taxonomy. **What remains unproven:** authentic byte release, session/chronological grouping, sufficient normal-only train/development/calibration partitions, useful causal warmup on short runs, and meaningful periodic state correspondence. The present single-reference observer may fail this domain before anomaly testing; such a failure should revise the claim.

## Benign variation and other robot sources

### NIST UR5 condition changes

The [NIST provider page](https://www.nist.gov/el/intelligent-systems-division-73500/degradation-measurement-robot-arm-position-accuracy) lists 18 controller CSVs covering half/full speed, 1.6/4.5 lb payload, cold-start conditions and numbered repeated tests, sampled at 125 Hz. It documents joint positions, velocities and additional controller quantities alongside externally measured positional accuracy. Its page began in 2017; catalogue release/modified fields differ and do not denote new 2026 recordings. The [catalogue](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/degradation-measurement-of-robot-arm-position-accuracy) links DOI `10.18434/M31962` and [NIST terms](https://www.nist.gov/open/license). No CSV or header workbook was downloaded, so exact schema, timing continuity and per-file license notices remain to be verified.

This could test whether a motion observer alarms on documented permissible speed/load changes. It does not provide matched authorized-versus-malicious interventions or demonstrate that our algorithm can infer intent from telemetry alone. Separate files at different settings are not proof of an observed within-run changeover. Fit per-source references on declared normal sessions; do not apply CASPER fitted means or period as if they described a UR5.

### UR5e stochastic pick-and-place

The [provider record](https://zenodo.org/records/5849300), metadata API and README confirm v0.0.0, 2022-01-24, open access and **CC BY-NC 4.0**. It offers a 2.6 GB archive plus documentation; no archive was read. The [experiment document](https://zenodo.org/api/records/5849300/files/ExperimentalDescription.pdf/content), pp. 1, 3–6, records UR5e software 5.9.1, RTDE, explicit loop/node registers, random branches/target positions and waits. Perturbations include physical pushes, altered payload, delayed waiting and reduced speed. Labels use a button and retrospective correction. A program node ID is extra task context, not one of our allowed q/qdot inputs.

The [peer-reviewed associated study](https://portal.findresearcher.sdu.dk/da/publications/an-experimental-comparison-of-anomaly-detection-methods-for-colla/) reports 600 nominal and 80 perturbed runs, DOI `10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3289068`, IEEE Access 2023. It is earlier than our recent-comparator window and remains necessary methodological background. The archive's internal scenario READMEs, raw schema, run chronology and exact published-file correspondence have not been inspected. This route would directly challenge fixed periodic progression; it requires a separately declared problem, not relabeling every application anomaly as an attack.

### AURSAD and FactoryNet overlap

[AURSAD's original provider](https://zenodo.org/records/4487073) describes 2,045 primary samples and 2,049 supplementary loosening/picking samples at 100 Hz; labels describe screw/assembly faults. The original API identifies CC BY 4.0. A newer [v1.1 record](https://zenodo.org/records/4559556) exists; its bytes and change log are not audited here. The [author loader](https://github.com/CptPirx/AURSAD) supports several label granularities and preprocessing options. Original and adapted releases cannot be interchanged without recording their mapping.

FactoryNet's [v5 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09081v5), revised 2026-10-08, differs substantially from its May/June versions. A workshop appearance is independently listed by [FMSD at ICML 2026](https://icml-structured-fm-workshop.github.io/accepted-papers/); this is **not an ICML main-conference publication claim**, and v5 is not thereby verified as the reviewed workshop version. Its [pinned data card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Forgis/FactoryNet/blob/3bea3cfc10545d898604f93170108576fd46d518/README.md) identifies original UR3/KUKA lab subsets, source-adapted voraus/AURSAD/CNC material and synthetic subsets. Lab/synthetic terms are MIT; adapted terms remain source-specific. It warns that the downloadable synthetic campaign is older than the reported five-arm campaign, recording context predicts some fault labels, and KUKA joint velocities are absent. No zero-filling of those channels would make it equivalent to our measurements. The current GitHub overview still advertises older totals/license framing; use pinned subset-level evidence, not a headline dataset size.

The HF metadata API confirms a public ungated repository at the pinned revision, not matching received data. No Parquet observations were fetched. Author claimed corpus counts and synthetic validation are not independently verified measurements. Repackaged AURSAD/voraus subsets must be deduplicated from their original sources in any evidence-breadth count.

## Admission and breadth requirements before a new experiment

1. **Choose the claim first.** A second fault dataset supports a robotic anomaly/observer claim; it does not turn the result into cross-robot cyberattack validation. A benign speed-change dataset tests false alarms, not malicious intent. Preserve this distinction in title, abstract and tables.
2. **Freeze an independent source receipt.** Provider/version/terms, original bytes/hashes, acquisition sessions, machine/task, faults versus attacks, units, clock resets and annotation uncertainty must be identified. Obtain only the bounded approved release. Exclude original/derivative overlaps by lineage and hashes.
3. **Create a source-specific contract.** CASPER's 1,528-record warmup, gap threshold of 0.075 seconds, 38-second period and continuous split counts do not transfer. At 100 Hz the warmup is 15.28 seconds, longer than the approximately 11-second average voraus execution. At 500 Hz it is 3.056 seconds, a different physical horizon. Use meaningful time-based initialization, verified native clocks, whole-run boundaries and causal scoring. Do not concatenate executions into a fictitious continuous motion cycle.
4. **Separate recordings before windows.** Prefer chronological sessions/whole executions for fitting, normal-only development, calibration and final tests. Verify whether provider IDs encode chronology; never infer chronology from arbitrary sample numbers. If collection order is unavailable, disclose grouped held-out execution evaluation rather than calling it chronological. Shared sessions and near-duplicate trajectories remain clustered observations.
5. **Declare what transfers.** Applying the same algorithm with a newly fitted per-dataset scaler/reference is across-source applicability, not zero-shot transfer. Genuine zero-shot claims require freezing the source model and documenting coordinate/units/robot differences. Do not transfer CASPER fitted state by default.
6. **Keep information access comparable.** Joint-state-only methods may underperform methods using torque, program nodes or commanded targets. Put those in a separate declared information track. Provider task/phase/anomaly labels must not become online detector inputs. Endpoint/truncation knowledge also needs explicit access rules.
7. **Retain independent units in reporting.** Rows and overlapping windows are not independent replications. With one CASPER acquisition, do not create population confidence by ordinary row bootstrap or treat the eight scheduled intervals as sampled robot deployments. Report per-interval outcomes and the limited case-study denominator when eventual final scoring is authorized. Broader uncertainty needs separately justified run/session sampling.
8. **Recheck observer correspondence before security tests.** Variable branches and spatial targets may invalidate a single circular reference. Decide a revised model/question on permitted normal development only; preserve negative results and freeze the revised protocol before any newly admitted final faults/attacks are inspected.

If a broad cyber claim is essential, a proposed independent testbed campaign should include separately restarted acquisition sessions, multiple nominal tasks and permitted speeds/payloads, matched commanded-motion interventions, trustworthy command/monitor logs, and reserved task/session conditions. Simulation can support a separately labeled mechanism test; it is not physical validation. Physical robot operation requires an authorized laboratory and its operating/safety procedure. No such testbed, hardware access or campaign is currently established, and none was run here.
